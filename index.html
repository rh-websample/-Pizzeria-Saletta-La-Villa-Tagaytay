Buonasera

Two people, one brick oven, and a house that used to just be home.

Saletta started the way most good trattorias do — with a kitchen that got a little too crowded for just the family. The red wooden villa on the ridge became the dining room. The garden became the herb bed. What didn't change was the idea: proper Italian food, made slowly, served to people who came up the mountain for exactly that.

Basil comes off the rack by the front door before it ever hits a pizza. Pasta gets rolled through a hand-crank machine a few hours before service. The oven runs on wood, the way it always has, and it doesn't much care what time you sat down — only that you're comfortable while you wait.

The Room

High beams, low candlelight, and the ridge air coming through the windows.

The dining porch runs the length of the house — old wood ceilings, wrought-iron chairs, and glass on every side so the Tagaytay breeze does most of the cooling. Good for a long lunch, better after dark.
